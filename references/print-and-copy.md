# Fixed-layout, print, and copy guidance

Read this reference for posters, packaging, in-box cards, presentations, contracts, PDFs, long images, and other fixed-layout material.

## Choose the physical scene

Start with the viewing context: storefront distance, handheld card, screen presentation, office printout, or production print. Set the real dimensions and reading distance before composing. The brand system controls the identity; the medium controls density, type size, margins, bleed, and export settings.

## Background and logo choice

- Deep burgundy with gold is the default expressive brand field.
- Use `brandmark-lockup.png` for a full brand statement and `badge.png` for a compact mark.
- The source system has separate gold, white, black, and wordmark variants. Use one only when the rights holder supplies it for the current task; do not recolor the packaged PNGs to approximate another official mark.
- Preserve transparency and aspect ratio. Provide at least one-quarter-logo-height clear space.
- Light paper `#fbf6ec` is reserved for documents and print contexts; it is not the default website background.

## Composition

- Build a few strong groups rather than many framed modules. Use scale, alignment, type, and one gold rule to create hierarchy.
- Keep full but breathable density. Avoid both empty luxury-signaling whitespace and crowded repetitions.
- Product imagery remains the only large source of full color. Crop deliberately and keep the shoe recognizable.
- A continuous long-image manual may use smooth tonal transitions, but that is a deliverable-specific decision—not a universal rule for posters, cards, or presentations.
- Do not reuse a page-based or long-image structure when the requested medium calls for a different reading rhythm.

## Copy

- Prefer one clear thought per panel or view.
- Use specific, understated phrasing. Let materials, craft, silhouette, and occasion carry the story.
- Keep claims traceable to the user's source. Never invent store counts, growth figures, prices, delivery times, awards, materials, or product capabilities.
- Avoid emoji, exclamation-heavy calls to action, and marketplace promotion language.

## Export preflight

- Confirm final width, height, orientation, and units.
- Confirm whether the printer requires bleed, crop marks, a color profile, or a particular PDF standard. Do not assume production settings.
- Check raster image effective resolution at final size and inspect transparent edges around the logo.
- Verify font embedding or outline requirements while preserving editable source when the workflow needs it.
- Render the final PDF or image and visually inspect every page or the complete long canvas for clipping, broken gradients, low-resolution images, seams, and text overflow.
- Distinguish a screen preview from a print-ready file in the handoff.
